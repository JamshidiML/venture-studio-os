# Non-clinical Wellbeing — Gate 1 Discovery

## Executive Summary

- **Twenty bounded candidates were documented without selecting a product.** The universe covers sleep routines, movement habits, meal organization, everyday stress self-management, digital breaks and neutral journaling.
- **Current evidence supports recurring contexts, not app effectiveness.** CDC/WHO sources establish that sleep difficulty and insufficient activity affect substantial populations, while federal guidance supports general healthy-routine concepts. None proves candidate demand, retention, WTP or health outcomes.
- **Safety and privacy narrow the usable space.** FDA’s 2026 general-wellness guidance separates lifestyle support from diagnosis/treatment; FTC, Google Play and Apple rules make health-data minimization, disclosure, consent and secure handling material even for wellness products.
- **The honest next step is independent Governance review.** This Gate 1 package contains no medical advice, candidate ranking, deep due diligence, customer validation, PRD or implementation.

## Methodology

Research prioritized public-health agencies, regulators and first-party platform policies. Candidates had to describe a repeated user-controlled workflow, fit the general-wellness boundary, identify an existing alternative, map to a dated source and state WTP/retention uncertainty. Clinical claims, medication, crisis response, eating-disorder optimization, medical devices, hidden sensor collection and other threads’ scopes were excluded.

## Evidence-backed findings

### Sleep routine friction is common, but causation remains unknown

CDC/NCHS reports that in 2024, 30.5% of U.S. adults slept under seven hours, 15.4% had trouble falling asleep and 18.1% had trouble staying asleep (W01). A separate report says 12.9% used sleep aids most days or every day (W02). **Inference:** routine planning and neutral context journaling merit discovery. **Boundary:** no candidate changes medication, diagnoses insomnia or claims to improve sleep.

### Movement candidates can use flexible public guidance without prescribing care

WHO estimates 31% of adults globally did not meet recommended activity levels in 2022 (W03). CDC states activity can be spread through the week and that some movement is better than none (W04). This supports lightweight reminders, walking plans and consistency logs as candidate workflows, not proof of adherence or health benefit.

### Meal organization is safer than individualized nutrition optimization

Current U.S. dietary guidance describes healthy eating patterns and nutrient-dense foods (W05). The retained candidates organize planning, pantry use and routines; they do not calculate therapeutic diets, treat disease, set universal hydration targets or promise weight outcomes.

### Everyday stress tools need a visible clinical and crisis boundary

CDC lists connection, healthy routines, breaks and relaxation techniques as coping approaches and advises professional help when distress persists or disrupts routine (W06). The archived Household Pulse Survey illustrates both the importance and measurement limitations of self-reported distress data (W12). Candidates therefore support reflection and user-chosen routines only, with no screening, diagnosis or crisis intervention.

### Data handling can be the binding constraint

FDA’s January 2026 guidance clarifies the line between general wellness and diagnosis/treatment (W07). FTC’s amended breach rule expressly reaches many health apps (W08). Google Play classifies sleep, nutrition, fitness and stress apps as health apps and requires declarations and privacy treatment (W09–W10); Apple treats health/medical data as sensitive (W11). **Implication:** local-first storage, deletion/export, minimal permissions and non-advertising use are candidate constraints, not optional polish.

## Risks, assumptions and unknowns

1. **Assumption:** user-controlled routine organization may provide value without making health claims. Owner: Strategy Agent. Impact if wrong: the organizational rationale cannot support advancement. Planned review/test: assess the assumption only in a separately authorized feasibility or validation issue.
2. **Assumption:** naturally recurring routines may create repeat use without punitive streaks. Owner: Strategy Agent. Impact if wrong: no repeat-use mechanism in this thread may support advancement or a retention claim. Planned review/test: only if a later Gate 5 issue separately authorizes validation, define a consented routine-frequency study before treating this as a hypothesis; no health, engagement, success, or kill threshold is authorized at Gate 1.
3. **Unknown:** candidate-specific WTP; prevalence is not payment evidence.
4. **Risk:** journals and routine data may be legally and personally sensitive even without a clinical feature.
5. **Risk:** users may over-rely on generated or generalized guidance; candidates must keep human judgment and help-seeking routes visible.

## Confidence assessment

Confidence is **high for the broad public-health and policy context**, **medium for the inference that repeated organizational workflows exist**, and **low for candidate-specific demand, WTP, retention, effect, localization and launch feasibility**. These layers remain intentionally separate.
